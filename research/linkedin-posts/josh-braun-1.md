For a long time, I thought influence came from being competent.  

Now I think it starts with curiosity.  

Think about a doctor.  

Doctor #1 walks into the room and says:  

“Your cholesterol is high. You need to change your diet.”  

Competent?  
Sure.  

Doctor #2 walks in and says:  

“Before we look at the numbers, how have you been feeling lately?”  

Same training.  
Same credentials.  

Different feeling.  

One starts with expertise.  
The other starts with curiosity.  

I learned this lesson the hard way.  

One of my best friends in college drank a lot.  

Not the way we all drank.  

He’d drink until he passed out.  

Because I cared about him, I decided I was going to fix him.  

So I gave him facts.  

I explained what alcohol was doing to his body.  

I gave him logic.  

I told him he was too smart to throw his future away.  

I built a pretty good case.  

And it didn’t work.  
He didn’t drink less.  
He drank more.  

And eventually he pulled away from me.  

Looking back, I approached him like Doctor #1.  

I led with answers.  

What I never said was:  

“Help me understand.”  

I was trying to change him before I understood him.  

That’s the trap a lot of us fall into.  

In friendships.  
In leadership.  
In sales.  

We think people trust us because we’re smart.  

More often, they trust us because they feel understood.  

And that starts with a question, not an answer.
