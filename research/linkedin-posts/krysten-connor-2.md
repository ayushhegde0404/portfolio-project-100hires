A seller I coach booked a meeting with an executive without sending a single follow-up. No "great connecting, let's find time." No chasing.   
  
The difference was a point of view.  
  
For six months she's been building a real opinion on her industry. Not a pitch. Not talking points. An actual stance on where the space is heading and what the smart players should do about it.  
  
So when she lands in front of executives, she's not angling for a reason to reach out later. She just shows up like a peer who sees their world clearly.  
  
One of them booked time on the spot at a conference. She moved a relationship with a chief R&D officer forward in a LinkedIn DM. (Her note is below.)  
  
Executives don't book meetings because we asked nicely. They book because we said something worth their time.  
  
A point of view is the thing that gets the meeting.  
  
If you want to start building yours:  
 - Pick one shift happening in your buyers' world based on the conversations you've had so far this year. Then use your customer success stories (either from deals you've won, or from anecdotes from your team, or the testimonials on your website) to create a real-life POV on how you help.   
 - Pressure-test that POV with your leadership. Once you validate it, practice saying it out loud until you can do it without notes. And until you can write it in less than two sentences in an email.   
 - Then when you speak to your customers or send them a message, lead with the POV, not the product. The product is just how you back it up.  
  
What's one opinion about your buyers' industry you'd be willing to defend in a room full of executives?   
  
And if you don't have one, now is the best time to start. You would be shocked (SHOCKED!) at how few sellers do this... and therefore how easy it is to differentiate yourself by putting in a little work. 😇
