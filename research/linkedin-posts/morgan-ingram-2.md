I analyzed 127 failed LinkedIn outreach attempts across SDR teams I've worked with. The pattern that kept showing up cost these companies hundreds of qualified meetings.  
   
First off… I have been there.  
   
Early in my sales career my messages were painful.  
   
Yes I used the "Hope all is well... My name is Morgan. We have synergies and I'd love to connect."  
   
Just absolutely terrible.  
   
But I realized something:  
   
They weren't ignoring ME....  
   
They were ignoring my APPROACH.  
   
That changed everything.  
   
So when I looked at the 127 messages of other reps.. I noticed they were doing the same thing as me.  
   
Here's the framework I developed after studying hundreds of messages:  
   
The AMP Observation Outbound Formula:  
   
↳ Observation (show them you know them)   
↳ Context (why this observation matters)   
↳ Pain point (what they're likely facing)   
↳ Value prop / Power Move (how you help)   
↳ Call to action (next step)  
   
Quick example:  
   
Before: "Hope all is well..." (almost barfed writing this)  
   
After: "hey.. saw your product launch at the conference last week and the AE/SDR openings. not sure if you have created sales messaging for the new product in your outbound yet or if it's still a work in progress. a CRO I know in your space doubled BDR headcount post-launch and still missed plan because the motion was not set.. curious how you're thinking about pipeline generation with the new product?"  
   
See the difference?  
   
The first one says nothing.  
   
The second one proves a human connected the dots between their product launch.. their hiring.. and a real problem they might be facing.  
   
Every successful message I've seen follows this pattern.  
   
As Samantha McKenna says "Show Them You KNOW Them" before you show them what you DO.  
   
When I follow this framework response rates jump.  
   
When SDR/AE teams I coach use this inside Sales Team Six they start more conversations on LinkedIn which leads to more pipeline.  
   
Your prospects are waiting for someone who gets them.  
   
The question is whether your team knows how to connect the dots to show up that way.
